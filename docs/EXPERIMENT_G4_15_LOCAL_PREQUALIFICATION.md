# G4.15 Typed Publication and Terminal-Phase Governance

G4.15 is a narrow exploratory successor to G4.14. It addresses the divergence
between deterministic probe success and visible semantic failure in the frozen
G4.14 qualification batch. It does not change the four scenarios, Baseline,
fixed model, seed policy, evaluator, or transcript-freezing contract.

## Frozen motivating evidence

- Run 548 published synthetic fallback language, including `On statement`,
  while all G4.14 probes passed.
- Run 550 changed an explicit staffing, monitoring, and rollback gap into
  unsupported claims that 24/7 coverage and dashboards were in place.
- Run 552 claimed to have reviewed a sprint backlog and engineering design
  document that were never supplied, then reopened candidate assessment after
  the interview had entered candidate questions.
- Run 554 claimed immediate containment work, promised another role's evidence
  capture, and assigned unregistered `Carlos Ruiz` as the incident owner.

## Mechanisms under test

1. The final public utterance is classified into typed `PublicationClaim`
   records containing actor, claim kind, object, predicate, temporal scope,
   evidence source, and authority scope.
2. Present operational actions, readiness assertions, and current-session
   artifact reviews require an accepted simulated-tool result; a model-authored
   intent label cannot authorize them.
3. A role cannot publish that another participant performed an operational
   action. Named owners are validated against the registered participant
   directory in final NPC and player speech, including appositive wording.
4. The final post-grounding publication pass receives the same protected-state,
   authority, evidence, task-type, and phase context as the earlier draft pass.
5. Once a structured interview enters `candidate_questions`, panel speech may
   answer the candidate but cannot reopen product, engineering, or leadership
   assessment.
6. Synthetic fallback templates are an explicit integrity failure. The last
   resort reply is natural, noncommittal, and contains no role or orchestration
   labels.
7. The G4.15 composite requires typed publication safety, terminal-phase
   monotonicity, natural fallback output, response ownership, direct routing,
   and terminal-floor integrity.
